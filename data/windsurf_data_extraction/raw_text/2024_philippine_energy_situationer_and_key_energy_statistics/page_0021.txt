The country’s crude oil import requirements were largely sourced from the Middle 
East, with Saudi Arabia’s hefty 51.4 percent share to total import volume, followed 
by the United Arab Emirates and Iraq, with shares of 30.9 percent and 13.4 percent, 
respectively. In contrast, refined petroleum products were largely imported from Asia. 
South Korea and China emerged as the Philippines’ top suppliers, a combined share 
of 53.4 percent of total refined product import, while Singapore also contributed 14.6 
percent share.
Crude oil exports increased by 4.8 percent to 67.0 kTOE compared to last year’s 63.9 
kTOE. Since operational adjustments necessitated temporary shutdown of Petron’s 
Bataan refinery, it led to the surplus in crude oil that was not processed domestically 
and instead exported to neighboring countries, such as Thailand. On the other hand, 
the reduced viability of the Malampaya gas field led to a 9.0 percent drop in condensate 
exports in 2024, driven by declining indigenous gas reserves and lower production 
output. In contrast, fulfillment of regional supply contracts boosted the export volume 
of finished petroleum products by 6.7 percent to 774.8 kTOE despite limited domestic 
refinery capacity.  The Philippines maintained its role as a regional supplier, with key 
export destinations that included China, Brunei, and Thailand.
Coal imports reached 21.3 million tons of oil equivalent (MTOE) in 2024 or 13.6 percent 
higher than its volume of 18.6 MTOE in 2023. This growth was primarily driven by 
the intensified use of coal in power generation. Indonesia continued to serve the 
Philippines as its dominant coal supplier (99.5 percent share), while the rest were 
sourced from Australia, China, Vietnam, South Africa, and South Korea. Exports of 
domestically produced coal climbed by 4.8 percent to 4.2 MTOE, which resulted from 
sustained high domestic coal output and elevated demand from overseas markets, as 
the combined demand from China and South Korea, the Philippines’ leading export 
markets, rose by 10.9 percent in 2024.
