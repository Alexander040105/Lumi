ENERGY-ECONOMY INDICATORS
ENERGY INTENSITY.  The country’s overall energy intensity28 slightly went down 
by 0.5 percent to 3.10 TOE/MPhP reflective of modest gains from stronger energy 
efficiency standards, technology adoption, and enforcement of regulations. Similarly, 
oil intensity registered a 1.6 percent decline to 7.47 bbl/PhP in 2024, driven by EV 
adoption and fleet modernization—and in industry through fuel switching. On the other 
hand, the amount of electricity use per unit of economic output rose from 5.61 Wh/PhP 
to 5.71 Wh/PhP due to higher consumption in energy-intensive industries, cold storage, 
and expanding EV use. These trends underscored the inverse trend between electricity 
and oil intensities, as electrification led to reduced oil reliance.
By sector, agriculture posted the sharpest rise in energy intensity of 10.9 percent to 
0.25 TOE/MPhp in 2024 brought about by increased mechanization, irrigation, and 
post-harvest processing. Industry and households registered intensity reductions of 
2.6 percent and 0.6 percent, respectively, while services recorded a modest 1.6 percent 
increase, driven by ICT, commercial, and tourism-related demand.

ENERGY ELASTICITY29. In 2024, economic output outperformed energy supply, 
resulting in an energy elasticity of 0.94 compared to 1.27 in 2023. It signified efficiency 
improvements, and highlighted the economic contribution of services sector, which was 
generally less-energy intensive. In contrast, oil elasticity increased to 0.70 and pointed 
to the rebound in fuel demand from heightened transport activity and industrial use. 
On the other hand, electricity elasticity climbed further to 1.33 in 2024, which implied 
that robust electricity demand was fueled by expanding electrification in transport and 
industry, the proliferation of cold storage facilities, and continued penetration of EVs. 
These movements highlight a structural transition from oil-based to electricity-based 
energy use.
