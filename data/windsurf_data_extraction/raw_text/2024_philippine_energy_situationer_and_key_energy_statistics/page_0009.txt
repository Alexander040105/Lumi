B. Total Final Energy Consumption by Sector
1. Transport
Energy consumption of the transport sector sustained its upward trajectory with a 5.2 
percent increase to 13.5 MTOE in 2024 from 12.9 MTOE in 2023. This was attributed to 
several key factors such as expanded infrastructure, increased vehicle ownership, and 
rising freight activity. Specifically, the surge in electric vehicle use, supported by zero-
tariff policies, also contributed to higher electricity demand and gradual adoption of 
cleaner technologies. 
Road transport’s energy consumption grew by 6.0 percent to 12.3 MTOE, with a 90.7 
percent share to the sector’s energy mix (Figure 5). The uptrend compensated for the 
decline in energy consumption across other transport subsectors and was supported 
by robust growth in vehicle sales, which increased by 8.7 percent in 2024, according 
to a joint report by the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines, 
Inc. (CAMPI) and the Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA)2. The rise in new vehicle 
registrations—which spanned private cars, commercial vehicles, and delivery fleets—
reflected growing mobility demand, urbanization, and increased economic activity.
Rail transport reported a 6.2 percent drop in energy use, from 11.9 kTOE in 2023 to 11.2 
in 2024, due to a double-digit reduction in both diesel and biodiesel of more than 40 
percent attributed to the suspension of Philippine National Railways (PNR) operations 
in Metro Manila3, which took effect on 28 March 2024. The five-year service halt is 
intended to expedite the construction of the North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR). 
On the other hand, the surge in passenger ridership for both Metro Rail Transit (MRT) 
and Light Rail Transit Authority (LRTA) systems – which reached over 189 million 
passengers in 2024 from 170 million in 20234 5 slightly cushioned the downtrend as 
electricity use for rail improved by 3.4 percent.
