Coal remained the dominant energy source with 35.7 percent share to TPES or 24.6 
MTOE, which was 7.3 percent more than its year-ago level of 23.0 MTOE. The volume 
of  local and imported coal ramped up by 4.3 percent and 8.9 percent, respectively, to 
provide for the demand from power and industrial processes. Aggregate renewable 
energy sources (geothermal, hydro, solar, wind, biomass, and biofuels) posted a 2.1 
percent expansion as it reached 21.0 MTOE or 30.4 percent share to the country’s 
energy mix. Meanwhile, total oil supply rose by 3.9 percent, from 19.6 MTOE in 2023 
to 20.4 MTOE in 2024, and contributed 29.5 percent to TPES. Bulk of the increase is 
attributed to the continued reliance on imported petroleum products, as domestic 
production remained limited. Meanwhile, natural gas supply significantly increased by 
21.0 percent boosted by the double-digit hike in LNG imnports during the year.
A. Indigenous Energy
The uptrend in domestic coal production and renewable energy sources, such as hydro, 
solar and biofuels, offset the decline in oil, natural gas and wind to keep the level of 
total indigenous energy production afloat at 31.0 MTOE or 1.7 percent higher than 30.5 
MTOE recorded in 2023. This was an improvement to the slowdown exhibited in 2023, 
and reflected the country’s continued efforts to strengthen energy security through 
aggressive promotion indigenous energy sources.
Fossil Fuels
i.	 Oil 
Combined production of crude oil and condensate fell by 9.4 percent to 241.2 kTOE (2.2 
million barrels (MMB)) in 2024 from 266.2 kTOE (2.4 MMB) in 2023. Galoc oil field, the 
country’s only commercially producing oil field during the year, recorded a 10.6 percent 
reduction in crude oil production due to aging infrastructure and the progressive 
depletion of its reservoir consistent with its late-life production phase. Condensate 
production from the Malampaya gas field, which is fully exported, also dropped by 
9.0 percent, consistent with the field’s ongoing decline in natural gas output and 
associated liquids.
ii.	 Coal
Coal production increased by 4.3 percent in 2024 to 8.4 MTOE (15.9 million metric tons 
(MMMT)) from 8.1 MTOE (15.3 MMMT) in the previous year. Semirara Mining and Power 
Corporation (SMPC), the country’s largest coal producer, sustained its production level 
at 14.7 MMT and accounted for 93.7 percent of total domestic production. Meanwhile, 
coal mines in South Cotabato, Sarrangi and Sultan Kudarat  and Zamboanga reported 
a surge of as much as four (4) times their combined production level in 2023 to 0.9 
MMMT and translated to a share of 5.5 percent to total domestic coal production. 
Small-scale mines and those in Cebu, Negros and Bicol contributed an additional 0.1 
MMMT or 0.8 percent share.
