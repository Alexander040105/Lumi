Industrial GHG emissions reached 13.0 MTCO2e in 2024 and translated to 8.0 percent 
of the total GHG emissions.  This sluggish uptick of 1.7 percent vis-à-vis 2023 level 
reflected the modest growth in industrial energy use, as well as increased adoption of 
cleaner technologies, and gradual shift toward less GHG emissions-intensive industries. 
Meanwhile, combined emissions from agriculture, services, and households increased 
by 7.7 percent to 11.1 MTCO2e from 10.3 MTCO2e, with a collective contribution of 6.9 
percent to the national GHG emission level. Additionally, emissions from refinery 
operations and own-use energy climbed 16.5 percent, from 1.5 MTCO2e to 1.8 MTCO2e.
IV. ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT 
In 2024, total greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions rose by 6.8 percent to 162.1 million 
tonnes of CO₂ equivalent (MTCO2e) from 151.8 MTCO2e in 2023. This increase was 
largely due to the sustained reliance on fossil fuels, particularly coal and oil, for power 
generation and transportation. The power sector remained as the largest GHG emitter 
with 60.1 percent share to the national total, as emissions from coal-fired plants surged 
by 8.0 percent to 97.5 MTCO2e in 2024 from the previous year’s 90.2 MTCO2e. (Table 1). 
GHG emission from the transport sector went up by 5.1 percent to 38.8 MTCO2e in 2024 
from last year’s 36.9 MTCO2e driven by economic growth and increased household 
consumption. The sector accounted for 23.9 percent of total GHG emissions, which is 
attributed to its continued dependence on internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles, 
which still comprised the largest share of the national vehicle fleet despite aggressive 
efforts to promote electric mobility.
