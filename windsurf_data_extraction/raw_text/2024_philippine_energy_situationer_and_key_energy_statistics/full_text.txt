B. Total Final Energy Consumption by Sector
1. Transport
Energy consumption of the transport sector sustained its upward trajectory with a 5.2 
percent increase to 13.5 MTOE in 2024 from 12.9 MTOE in 2023. This was attributed to 
several key factors such as expanded infrastructure, increased vehicle ownership, and 
rising freight activity. Specifically, the surge in electric vehicle use, supported by zero-
tariff policies, also contributed to higher electricity demand and gradual adoption of 
cleaner technologies. 
Road transport’s energy consumption grew by 6.0 percent to 12.3 MTOE, with a 90.7 
percent share to the sector’s energy mix (Figure 5). The uptrend compensated for the 
decline in energy consumption across other transport subsectors and was supported 
by robust growth in vehicle sales, which increased by 8.7 percent in 2024, according 
to a joint report by the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines, 
Inc. (CAMPI) and the Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA)2. The rise in new vehicle 
registrations—which spanned private cars, commercial vehicles, and delivery fleets—
reflected growing mobility demand, urbanization, and increased economic activity.
Rail transport reported a 6.2 percent drop in energy use, from 11.9 kTOE in 2023 to 11.2 
in 2024, due to a double-digit reduction in both diesel and biodiesel of more than 40 
percent attributed to the suspension of Philippine National Railways (PNR) operations 
in Metro Manila3, which took effect on 28 March 2024. The five-year service halt is 
intended to expedite the construction of the North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR). 
On the other hand, the surge in passenger ridership for both Metro Rail Transit (MRT) 
and Light Rail Transit Authority (LRTA) systems – which reached over 189 million 
passengers in 2024 from 170 million in 20234 5 slightly cushioned the downtrend as 
electricity use for rail improved by 3.4 percent.

2. Households
In 2024, the household sector maintained its position as the second-largest energy-
consuming sector in the Philippines with its 28.2 percent share to TFEC. Energy use for 
household activities increased by 4.3 percent to 11.0 MTOE from 10.5 MTOE in 2023. This 
upward trend was largely attributed to enhanced access to energy services, driven by 
the government’s sustained electrification initiatives, expanded availability of clean 
cooking fuels, and programs promoting energy efficiency at the household level.
Biomass remained the primary fuel source,  with fuelwood accounting for 37.8 
percent of household energy use (Figure 6), followed by charcoal at 13.0 percent and 
agricultural waste at 4.4 percent. Although total biomass consumption grew slightly 
by 1.0 percent from 6.0 MTOE in 2023 to 6.1 MTOE in 2024, its share to the household 
energy mix declined by 1.8 percentage points. This shift reflected a gradual transition 
toward cleaner and more modern fuels, such as electricity and LPG, driven by improved 
electrification and wider access to clean cooking technologies.
Successfully electrified over 2,000,000 additional household reported by the 
Distribution Utilities (DUs) as of the first half of 2024, the Department of Energy (DOE) 
continued the implementation of its Total Electrification Program10. This development 
led to an 11.5 percent increase in household electricity consumption, which reached 
3.5 MTOE in 2024 and accounted for 32.3 percent of the household energy mix. The 
increased adoption of electricity, recognized as a cleaner and more efficient fuel, 
reflected a growing preference for modern energy sources and marked a shift away 
from traditional fuels.

Energy consumption in the agriculture sector reached 438.8 kTOE in 2024 or 9.3 percent 
higher than its 401.6 kTOE in 2023, reflected a share of 1.1 percent of the country’s 
TFEC. The hike in the sector’s energy use mirrored the shift to mechanized operations, 
expanded livestock farming, as well as resilient harvests that required processing and 
transport and increased use of cold storage and processing facilities.

i.	 Oil

to the previous year’s 5.5 percent. Despite this modest acceleration, the country 
ranked as the second fastest growing economy in the region – next to Vietnam and 
ahead of its other regional peers23. This underscored the domestic economy’s ability 
to sustain growth amidst persistent external challenges, including subdued global 
demand, climate-related disruptions, and geopolitical tensions reminiscent of 2023.

engines of growth for the year, expanding by 6.7 percent and 5.6 percent, respectively.

other hand, the agriculture sector contracted by 1.5 percent, weighed down by back-
to-back typhoons26 that impacted northern Luzon, coupled with the prevalence of 
animal diseases that disrupted the sector’s value chains during the year.

ENERGY INTENSITY.  The country’s overall energy intensity28 slightly went down 
by 0.5 percent to 3.10 TOE/MPhP reflective of modest gains from stronger energy 
efficiency standards, technology adoption, and enforcement of regulations. Similarly, 
oil intensity registered a 1.6 percent decline to 7.47 bbl/PhP in 2024, driven by EV 
adoption and fleet modernization—and in industry through fuel switching. On the other 
hand, the amount of electricity use per unit of economic output rose from 5.61 Wh/PhP 
to 5.71 Wh/PhP due to higher consumption in energy-intensive industries, cold storage, 
and expanding EV use. These trends underscored the inverse trend between electricity 
and oil intensities, as electrification led to reduced oil reliance.
By sector, agriculture posted the sharpest rise in energy intensity of 10.9 percent to 
0.25 TOE/MPhp in 2024 brought about by increased mechanization, irrigation, and 
post-harvest processing. Industry and households registered intensity reductions of 
2.6 percent and 0.6 percent, respectively, while services recorded a modest 1.6 percent 
increase, driven by ICT, commercial, and tourism-related demand.

Glossary
Condensate
Liquid hydrocarbons separated from gas 
production.
Dependable  
Capacity 
The capacity that can be relied upon to carry 
system load for a specified time interval and 
period, provide assumed reserve, and/or meet 
firm power obligations.
Electrification
Electrification is either done through grid or off-
grid connection.  When a barangay is provided 
with electricity through grid connection, it 
means that the distribution line has reached the 
barangay proper.  It may also mean that almost 
50.0 percent of potential households in the 
barangay are connected to a distribution utility 
(DU) (i.e. MERALCO) or at least one is connected 
to other DUs. Off-grid connection pertains to 
a barangay having about 20 to 30 households 
availing the connection.
Energy Intensity
Calculated as units of energy (million tons of 
oil equivalent, MTOE) per unit of GDP (in billion 
pesos).
Energy Per Capita
Amount of energy used per person. It is 
calculated as total primary energy demand (in 
MTOE) over population (in millions).
Energy Self 
Sufficiency
The ratio of the country's domestic energy 
supply to total supply; measures the degree at 
which domestic energy forms can support total 
energy demand.
Energy to GDP 
Elasticity
The percentage change in energy supply to 
achieve one per cent change in national GDP. 
Calculated as the ratio of growth of primary 
energy demand over GDP growth.
Gas (or Natural Gas)
A naturally occurring mixture of hydrocarbon 
and non-hydrocarbon gases in porous 
formations beneath the earth's surface, often 
in association with petroleum. The principal 
constituent is methane.

KEY ENERGY STATISTICS
75
Geothermal Energy
Energy generated by heat stored in the earth, 
or the collection of absorbed heat derived from 
underground in the atmosphere and oceans.
Gross Domestic 
Product (GDP)
Total market value of all final goods and services 
produced within the country in a given period 
of time (usually a calendar year), or the sum 
of value added of all final goods and services 
produced within a country in a given period of 
time.
Gross National 
Product (GNP)
The value of all (final) goods and services 
produced in a country in one year, plus income 
earned by its citizens abroad, minus income 
earned by foreigners in the country.
Hydropower
"Also called hydraulic power or water power; 
derived from the force or energy of moving water, 
which may be harnessed for useful purposes.
"
Indigenous Energy
Refers to all energy forms produced/sourced 
from within the country's natural resources.
Installed Capacity
The total of the capacities shown on the 
nameplates of the generating units in a 
powerplant.
Renewable Energy 
Energy generated from natural resources which 
are naturally replenished. It includes solar power, 
wind power, hydroelectricity, micro hydro, 
biomass and biofuels.
Run of Mine
Coal directly coming from the mine
Total Final Energy 
Consumption (TFEC)
The sum of all energy forms consumed/used by 
different economic sectors
Total Primary Energy 
Demand (TPED)
The sum of total final consumption, power 
generation, other energy sector (own use and 
losses).
Total Primary Energy 
Supply (TPES)
The sum of all energy derived from domestic 
sources (indigenous, renewable), imported from 
outside the country, stock change (+/-) and 
export (-)
