In 2024, TPES rose to 69.0 MTOE, a 5.2 percent increase from 65.6 MTOE in 2023, driven 
by stronger energy demand and expanded power generation. Although the growth 
in net energy imports slowed to 8.2 percent in 2024 vis-à-vis 13.9 in 2023, it remained 
substantial with 55.0 percent share to TPES at 38.0 MTOE. It compensated for the 
sluggish 1.7 percent increment in domestic energy production as it reached 31.0 
MTOE in 2024 from 30.5 MTOE the previous year. As a result, energy self-sufficiency 
declined further by 1.6 percentage points to 45.0 percent from 46.5 percent in 2023 
(Figure 14).
III. TOTAL PRIMARY ENERGY 
SUPPLY (TPES)
Power plants’ total fuel input rose by 6.0 percent to 38.9 MTOE in 2024, from 36.7 MTOE 
in 2023. Due to higher demand from baseload operations, coal consumption for power 
generation went up by 7.3 percent to 22.4 MTOE and accounted for 57.6 percent of the 
total fuel input (Figure 13). Natural gas increased by 13.9 percent to 2.8 MTOE from 
2.5 MTOE in 2023 spurred by LNG imports and additional gas-fired capacity that came 
online during the year. Geothermal energy input remained relatively stable at 9.3 MTOE, 
while hydro increased slightly by 6.0 percent to 2.7 MTOE during the period. Solar and 
wind inputs for power generation mirrored their generation output growth trend, with 
inputs of 0.3 MTOE and 0.1 MTOE, respectively. Meanwhile, biomass consumption for 
power applications rose modestly by 2.5 percent from 0.5 MTOE in 2023 to 0.6 MTOE 
in 2024.
