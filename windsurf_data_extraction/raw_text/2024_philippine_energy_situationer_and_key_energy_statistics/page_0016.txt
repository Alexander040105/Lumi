The volume of domestically refined diesel and gasoline, with an aggregate share of 
64.1 percent to the country’s refinery mix, contracted by 12.5 percent and 0.9 percent, 
respectively, as imports met a larger share of market demand. LPG (3.8 percent 
share), kerosene (0.3 percent share) and naphtha (0.3 percent share) exhibited the 
same downtrend with negative growths of 2.4 percent, 7.2 percent and 87.9 percent, 
respectively. Aggregate output of other petroleum products, which accounted for 12.9 
percent share, also fell by 10.4 percent. In contrast, refinery output for jet kerosene 
(0.3. percent share) improved by 12.8 percent, while that of fuel oil (0.9 percent share) 
increased by 20.7 percent.
B. Power Generation and Fuel Input
Aggregate power generation reached 126.9 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2024, 7.6 percent 
more than its 2023 level of 118.0 TWh. The increase was in response to robust economic 
performance and continued expansion in electricity demand across sectors during the 
year.
Coal-fired power plants remained the dominant source of supply, accounting for 62.5 
percent (Figure 13) of the total generation mix or 79.4 TWh in 2024, a significant 
increase of 7.6 percent from its 2023 level of 73.8 TWh. Natural gas power generation 
also grew steadily, contributing 18.0 TWh or 14.2 percent of total generation, up from 
its previous year’s level of 16.7 TWh. Oil-based power plants also posted increased in 
their generation output at 2.9 percent. 
Meanwhile, renewable energy sources supplied 22.2 percent of the total generation 
output in 2024. Geothermal and hydropower, with combined shares of 17.1 percent, each 
contributed more than 10 TWh. Solar energy showed a notable 49.8 percent growth, 
as it produced 3.8 TWh of power. Wind power dropped by 5.3 percent to 1.2 TWh with 
only 1.0 percent share, while biomass generation stood at 1.4 TWh, equivalent to a 1.1 
percent share of the generation mix.
