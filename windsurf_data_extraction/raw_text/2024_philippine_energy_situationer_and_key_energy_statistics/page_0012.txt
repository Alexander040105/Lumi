3. Industry
Aggregate industrial output sped up by 5.6 percent in 2024 vis-à-vis 3.6 percent in 2023, 
and contributed 29.1 percent share to GDP. This acceleration was primarily driven by 
robust performances in the construction and manufacturing sectors. Correspondingly, 
the sector’s energy consumption rebounded by 2.7 percent to 7.3 MTOE, reversing the 
0.5 percent decline recorded in the previous year.

Despite the 5.3 percent hike in approved permits for residential and commercial 
buildings11, energy use in construction declined by 12.8 percent due to double-
digit reduction in diesel utilization. Ths implied reduced reliance on diesel-powered 
equipment, and a shift to electric-powered and fuel-efficient machinery, as well as 
other alternative construction technologies.
On the other hand, despite the sluggish performance of the mining sub-sector, energy 
consumption went up by 5.2 percent to 596.3 kTOE in 2024 from its year-ago level of 
566.8 kTOE. This is attributed to sustained energy use in extraction and processing 
requirements to address operational challenges such as adverse weather conditions 
and logistical issues.
Electricity accounted for the largest share in the sector’s aggregate energy demand 
mix at 36.8 percent. Its aggregate consumption went up by 5.4 percent to 2.7 MTOE 
in 2024 (Figure 8) from 2.5 MTOE in the previous year due to its extensive use 
across production activities and processes. Lower coal prices contributed to the 8.0 
percent rise in coal consumption during the year, as levels reached 2.1 MTOE from
